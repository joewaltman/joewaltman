What I do

I build software that removes repetitive manual work from operations. The intake, quoting, booking, and follow-through that pile up when work arrives from outside a business through channels the business does not control.

I spent the past year deep in AI development, building things and working out where it is genuinely useful and where it is noise. Most of what gets marketed as AI for operations solves a problem that was never expensive. The work actually worth automating tends to be unglamorous: the same document produced fifty different ways for fifty different customers, the follow-up that never happens, the number nobody can see across the whole business.

My focus is manufacturers, logistics operators, and construction firms. Smaller companies whose work arrives from larger, more automated counterparties on terms the smaller party does not set.

What I’m working on now

September 2026

The same pattern keeps showing up in different industries. Work arrives from outside a business through a portal, an inbox, or a phone call, and someone inside re-types it into whatever system the company runs on. The volume climbs, because the larger party on the other end is automating faster than the smaller one can absorb. Off-the-shelf software does not solve it, because the work is specific to each counterparty and there is no recurring license in it, so no vendor builds it.

In manufacturing that shows up in quotes arriving through customer procurement systems. In logistics it is booking requests coming in through half a dozen channels. In construction it is the paperwork a smaller firm has to produce inside every large client’s system.

If you run an operation like this, I would like to hear about it.
